# Spield PT/YT AMM Trading and Yield-Settlement Specification

**Status:** Architecture draft for implementation and security review  
**Protocol:** Spield fixed-income markets on Stellar/Soroban  
**Underlying example:** USDC supplied to Blend  
**Token model:** PT as a transferable Stellar asset through SAC; YT as an SAC asset whose direct wallet-to-wallet transfers are unsupported in the initial release  
**Scope:** PT trading, capital-efficient YT trading, partial YT exits, yield claiming, maturity settlement, pool liquidity, failure handling, security invariants, and acceptance tests

> This document defines the intended system behavior. It is not a substitute for a formal economic review, smart-contract audit, or adversarial test campaign.

---

## 1. Executive summary

Each maturity series splits one unit of deposited underlying value into:

- **1 PT:** the bearer claim on one unit of principal at maturity.
- **1 YT:** the claim on yield generated by that unit until the series settles.

The initial market contains only **PT and USDC**. PT trades directly against USDC. YT is traded indirectly through the relationship:

```text
1 PT + 1 YT = claim on 1 unit of series underlying
```

Capital-efficient YT entry and exit must work as follows:

```text
Buy YT  = user contribution + AMM USDC contribution
          → mint PT to AMM and YT to user

Sell YT = AMM contributes matching PT
          → burn PT + user's YT
          → redeem underlying
          → AMM retains PT value and pays YT value to user
```

The user does **not** need to temporarily provide the entire principal. If 100 PT are worth 94 USDC, buying 100 YT should cost approximately 6 USDC plus fees and slippage. The AMM provides the remaining 94 USDC and atomically receives 100 PT.

There is no outstanding loan after the transaction and therefore no ordinary collateral liquidation. There is, however, **liquidity risk**: early YT entry or exit may become expensive or unavailable if the AMM lacks the required USDC or PT reserve.

The protocol must preserve the following separation:

```text
Backing and solvency             → Wrapper + Blend strategy
Yield accrual and claims         → Wrapper + Blend strategy
PT redemption at maturity       → Wrapper + Blend strategy
Early PT and YT price discovery  → PT/USDC AMM
```

If the AMM is unavailable, early trading may stop. Earned-yield claims, final YT settlement, and PT redemption must remain available independently of AMM liquidity, subject to the underlying Blend and USDC risks.

## 2. Terminology

### 2.1 Underlying

The asset deposited by users, such as USDC.

### 2.2 Strategy asset

The claim produced when the wrapper supplies the underlying to Blend. Spield should use Blend's real on-chain rate/index rather than a fabricated APY.

### 2.3 Series

A PT/YT market identified by at least:

```text
underlying asset
strategy
maturity or settlement rule
PT token address
YT token address
wrapper address
market address
```

Tokens and positions from different series must never be combined.

### 2.4 PT

A transferable bearer claim on principal. Ownership is determined by PT token balance, not by the depositor's original position record.

### 2.5 YT

A claim on yield generated before the series settlement point. In the initial SAC design, YT is economically associated with the wrapper's yield position for the holder.

### 2.6 Yield position

The wrapper's accounting record for a user's active YT amount, last settled yield index, and unpaid claimable yield.

### 2.7 Early trade

A PT or YT buy/sell performed before the market trading cutoff and series settlement.

### 2.8 Settlement

The transition that fixes the final yield index for the series, stops further YT accrual, disables new YT entry, and enables final redemption paths.

## 3. System components and responsibilities

## 3.1 Series wrapper

The wrapper is responsible for:

- Accepting underlying deposits.
- Supplying underlying to Blend.
- Tracking global backing and liabilities.
- Minting equal PT and YT notional for a split.
- Maintaining YT yield positions.
- Calculating yield from the real strategy index.
- Checkpointing and paying earned yield.
- Burning matching PT and YT during early merge/exit.
- Redeeming PT at maturity.
- Recording the final series settlement index.
- Preventing cross-series or mismatched-amount redemption.

The wrapper must not use the AMM price to calculate protocol backing or earned yield.

## 3.2 PT SAC

PT remains a normal Stellar asset exposed through SAC. It may be:

- Transferred between users.
- Held in wallets.
- Deposited into the Soroban AMM.
- Bought and sold before maturity.
- Redeemed by the current bearer at maturity.

No original-depositor position is required for PT redemption.

## 3.3 YT SAC

The initial YT remains a SAC token for compatibility and implementation simplicity. The wrapper may mint and burn it, but SAC transfers cannot execute a custom Spield yield checkpoint.

Consequently:

- YT may be minted directly to a user through Spield.
- YT may be burned directly from a user through an authorized Spield operation.
- Direct wallet-to-wallet YT transfer is unsupported.
- A direct transfer does not correctly transfer the wrapper's yield position.
- The app must not expose a generic YT transfer feature.
- Claims and protocol-mediated sells must verify that the position remains backed by the required YT balance.

This is a product restriction, not a claim that the SAC technically prevents all transfers.

## 3.4 PT/USDC market

The AMM is responsible for:

- Direct PT buys and sells.
- Capital-efficient YT buys and sells.
- Determining early-market prices, fees, and slippage.
- Enforcing reserve floors and trade-size limits.
- Preventing its invariant from decreasing except for explicitly defined LP operations.
- Preventing a trade from crossing configured time/yield/price bounds.

The AMM is not responsible for:

- Paying accrued yield.
- Determining Blend yield.
- Guaranteeing early exit liquidity.
- Redeeming PT at maturity.
- Covering a Blend loss or USDC depeg.

## 3.5 Blend strategy adapter

The adapter is responsible for:

- Supplying and withdrawing underlying from the selected Blend pool.
- Reading the authoritative strategy index/rate.
- Converting strategy shares and underlying with consistent rounding.
- Reporting available withdrawal liquidity separately from accounting value when possible.
- Surfacing pauses, failed withdrawals, and loss conditions to the wrapper.

## 4. Non-negotiable protocol invariants

The following properties must hold after every successful transaction.

### 4.1 Equal split invariant

Every new split of `N` underlying creates exactly:

```text
N PT
N YT
```

No function may mint only one side without an explicitly audited liability adjustment.

### 4.2 Matching merge invariant

Before settlement, redeeming `N` units of principal backing through a merge requires:

```text
N valid PT from the same series
N active YT notional from the same series
```

The wrapper must burn both sides or otherwise irrevocably remove both liabilities.

### 4.3 Global solvency invariant

The wrapper must track global series backing because a PT holder may have no relationship with the position that originally created the PT.

Conceptually:

```text
recoverable strategy assets + liquid underlying
    >= principal liabilities + already-realized unpaid yield liabilities
```

The exact formula must include strategy-share rounding, realized losses, fees, and any reserve buffer.

### 4.4 One authoritative yield ledger

For the SAC-YT release, the wrapper's yield-position ledger is authoritative. The YT token must not maintain a second independent interest ledger.

### 4.5 Checkpoint-before-amount-change invariant

Before increasing, decreasing, merging, burning, or closing a user's active YT amount, the wrapper must calculate yield using the **old amount** and the current strategy index.

```text
checkpoint old amount
update settled index
then change amount
```

Reversing this order can erase earned yield or pay yield on an amount that was not held during the accrual period.

### 4.6 Claims independent of AMM invariant

Yield claims must be paid or credited by the wrapper from strategy backing. Claiming must not withdraw value from AMM reserves.

### 4.7 Maturity redemption independent of AMM invariant

PT redemption and final YT settlement must not require a PT/USDC swap.

### 4.8 Atomic trade invariant

A capital-efficient YT trade must either complete all legs or complete none of them. There must never be a state where:

- The pool contributed USDC but did not receive PT.
- YT was minted without full backing.
- PT was burned but underlying was not accounted for.
- A position amount changed but the corresponding YT burn failed.
- The user paid but did not receive the required output.

### 4.9 Series isolation invariant

Each market may accept only its configured:

- Underlying.
- PT.
- YT.
- Wrapper.
- Strategy.
- Maturity series.

### 4.10 No negative balances or negative claimable yield

Rounding or adverse rate movement must never underflow balances or create a negative claimable amount. Loss handling must use an explicit loss/insolvency path.

## 5. Recommended yield-position model

For the initial non-transferable-in-practice YT design, the cleanest model is one aggregate position per `(series_id, user)`.

```rust
struct YieldPosition {
    owner: Address,
    series_id: BytesN<32>,
    active_yt: i128,
    settled_index: i128,
    claimable_yield: i128,
    total_claimed: i128,
}
```

The wrapper should also track:

```rust
struct SeriesAccounting {
    total_pt_liability: i128,
    total_active_yt: i128,
    total_claimable_yield: i128,
    backing_strategy_shares: i128,
    liquid_underlying: i128,
    settlement_index: Option<i128>,
    state: SeriesState,
}
```

### Why aggregate per user and series

If a user buys YT multiple times:

1. Checkpoint the existing amount.
2. Preserve the resulting `claimable_yield`.
3. Increase `active_yt`.
4. Set `settled_index` to the current index.

This prevents new YT from earning historical yield while avoiding dozens of independent positions that must later be selected and reconciled.

If the existing implementation retains separate `position_id` values, every sell and claim must specify which positions are affected, and the protocol must ensure that the same SAC balance is not counted as backing multiple position claims.

### Balance consistency check

At minimum, before a claim or sell:

```text
user YT balance >= user's total active YT liability for that series
```

Checking only one position at a time is insufficient if the user owns several positions, because the same token balance could otherwise appear to back several independent claims.

## 6. Pricing model

## 6.1 Economic identity

For a fully backed, lossless series before fees:

```text
P(PT) + P(YT) ≈ P(series underlying claim)
```

If the underlying unit is normalized to 1 USDC:

```text
P(YT) ≈ 1 - P(PT)
```

This is an economic relationship, not permission to hardcode the YT price. The actual execution price must include:

- AMM curve movement.
- Trade size.
- Reserve imbalance.
- Time to maturity.
- Fees.
- Rounding.
- Any configured price/yield range.
- Available strategy withdrawal liquidity for exits.

## 6.2 Exact-output YT purchase quote

For a requested `N` YT:

1. Ask the AMM curve how much USDC it would output if `N` newly minted PT were sold into the pool.
2. Call that value `pt_sale_value`.
3. Calculate:

```text
user_usdc_in = N - pt_sale_value + YT fees
pool_usdc_contribution = N - user_usdc_in
```

For example:

```text
Requested YT:              100
AMM value of 100 PT:        94 USDC
Fee and rounding:         0.20 USDC
User maximum input:        6.20 USDC
Pool net contribution:    93.80 USDC
```

The UI must show a live quote. It must never guarantee that 100 YT always costs exactly 6 USDC.

## 6.3 Exact-input YT sale quote

For `N` YT being sold:

1. Calculate how much USDC the AMM must retain in exchange for removing `N` PT from its reserve.
2. Merge the user's `N` YT with the AMM's `N` PT to release `N` underlying.
3. Calculate:

```text
user_usdc_out = N - value_retained_by_AMM - YT fees
```

## 6.4 Time-aware behavior

PT must converge toward its redeemable value as maturity approaches, while YT's remaining-time value approaches zero.

```text
Far from maturity: PT < 1 and YT > 0
At settlement:     PT principal claim is fixed and future YT value = 0
```

A plain constant-product AMM does not force this convergence. The production design should use a time-aware implied-yield curve or, for an MVP, strict maturity-based price bounds and an early trading cutoff.

AMM price must never be used as proof that the wrapper is solvent.

## 7. Direct PT trading

## 7.1 Buy PT with exact USDC input

Suggested interface:

```rust
buy_pt_exact_in(
    user: Address,
    usdc_in: i128,
    min_pt_out: i128,
    deadline_ledger: u32,
) -> i128
```

Flow:

1. Verify the market is open for PT trading.
2. Verify the deadline.
3. Require user authorization.
4. Calculate `pt_out` from current reserves and curve.
5. Verify `pt_out >= min_pt_out`.
6. Transfer USDC from user to market.
7. Transfer PT from market to user.
8. Update stored reserves consistently.
9. Verify the post-trade invariant.
10. Emit a PT purchase event.

Effect on reserves:

```text
PT reserve decreases
USDC reserve increases
```

## 7.2 Sell PT for exact PT input

Suggested interface:

```rust
sell_pt_exact_in(
    user: Address,
    pt_in: i128,
    min_usdc_out: i128,
    deadline_ledger: u32,
) -> i128
```

Flow:

1. Verify the market and deadline.
2. Require user authorization.
3. Calculate `usdc_out`.
4. Verify `usdc_out >= min_usdc_out`.
5. Verify the USDC reserve floor remains satisfied.
6. Transfer PT from user to market.
7. Transfer USDC from market to user.
8. Update reserves and verify the invariant.
9. Emit a PT sale event.

Effect on reserves:

```text
PT reserve increases
USDC reserve decreases
```

PT trading does not interact with the seller's original deposit position.

## 8. Capital-efficient YT purchase

## 8.1 Required behavior

The user provides only the quoted YT price. The pool provides the rest of the underlying required for the split and receives the newly minted PT.

Suggested interface:

```rust
buy_yt_exact_out(
    user: Address,
    yt_out: i128,
    max_usdc_in: i128,
    deadline_ledger: u32,
) -> i128
```

An exact-input alternative may also be provided:

```rust
buy_yt_exact_in(
    user: Address,
    usdc_in: i128,
    min_yt_out: i128,
    deadline_ledger: u32,
) -> i128
```

## 8.2 Recommended Soroban call direction

```text
User → Market.buy_yt_exact_out() → Wrapper.split_for_market()
```

The wrapper may call the USDC, PT, YT, and Blend contracts. It must not call the market's `buy_yt` function back.

This avoids:

```text
Market → Wrapper → Market
```

and avoids a callback/re-entry collision.

## 8.3 Detailed transaction flow

For `N` YT:

1. Verify `N > 0` and above the dust threshold.
2. Verify the series is open and before the YT trading cutoff.
3. Verify the deadline.
4. Require user authorization.
5. Read market reserves and the time-aware curve state.
6. Quote the value of adding `N` PT to the pool.
7. Calculate the user's required USDC input.
8. Verify `required_usdc <= max_usdc_in`.
9. Verify the pool contribution does not breach its USDC reserve floor.
10. Verify `N` is below the maximum trade-size limit.
11. Checkpoint the user's existing YT position, if any.
12. Transfer the user's USDC contribution to the market or directly into the controlled split flow.
13. Add the required USDC contribution from the market reserve.
14. Supply exactly `N` total USDC to the wrapper.
15. Deposit the underlying into the configured Blend strategy.
16. Mint exactly `N` PT directly to the configured market.
17. Mint exactly `N` YT directly to the user.
18. Increase the user's active YT position by `N` at the current strategy index.
19. Increase global PT and active-YT liabilities by `N`.
20. Update AMM reserves as if the newly minted PT had been sold into the pool.
21. Verify token balance deltas and the post-trade invariant.
22. Emit split, position, and YT purchase events.

## 8.4 Example

```text
User requests:             100 YT
AMM values 100 PT at:       94 USDC
Fees:                     0.20 USDC

User contributes:         6.20 USDC
AMM contributes:         93.80 USDC
Wrapper receives:       100.00 USDC

Wrapper mints:
100 PT → AMM
100 YT → User
```

Final result:

```text
User: pays 6.20 USDC and receives 100 YT
AMM:  gives 93.80 USDC and receives 100 PT
Wrapper: receives full backing and creates both liabilities
```

The AMM's contribution is not an unsecured loan. It receives PT atomically. No user debt or health factor remains after the transaction.

## 8.5 Failure behavior

The entire transaction must revert if:

- The user refuses the final authorization.
- The quote exceeds `max_usdc_in`.
- AMM reserves are insufficient.
- The reserve floor would be breached.
- Blend supply fails.
- Either token mint fails.
- The position update fails.
- The post-trade invariant fails.
- The series becomes paused or matured during simulation/execution.

No partially minted or partially funded state may remain.

## 9. Full and partial YT sales

## 9.1 Required behavior

Selling YT closes some or all of the user's future-yield exposure. It does not transfer the YT to another user.

The AMM provides matching PT, the wrapper settles the seller's accrued yield, and matching PT/YT are burned to release underlying.

Suggested interface:

```rust
sell_yt_exact_in(
    user: Address,
    yt_in: i128,
    min_usdc_out: i128,
    claim_mode: ClaimMode,
    deadline_ledger: u32,
) -> SellYtResult
```

Possible claim modes:

```rust
enum ClaimMode {
    CreditOnly,
    PayClaimableNow,
}
```

`CreditOnly` checkpoints earned yield without forcing an immediate yield withdrawal. `PayClaimableNow` checkpoints and attempts to pay all available claimable yield in the same transaction.

## 9.2 Detailed partial-sale flow

Suppose the user has 100 YT and sells 50 YT.

1. Verify `yt_in = 50` is positive and not greater than the user's active YT amount.
2. Verify the market permits YT exits.
3. Verify the deadline.
4. Require user authorization.
5. Confirm the user's YT balance backs the user's total active YT liability.
6. Read the current strategy index.
7. Checkpoint accrued yield using the **full old amount of 100 YT**.
8. Add the result to `claimable_yield`.
9. Set `settled_index` to the current index.
10. Quote the USDC output for selling 50 YT.
11. Verify `usdc_out >= min_usdc_out`.
12. Verify the AMM has at least 50 usable PT after respecting its PT reserve floor.
13. Move or authorize exactly 50 AMM PT into the wrapper merge path.
14. Burn exactly 50 YT from the user.
15. Reduce the user's `active_yt` from 100 to 50.
16. Reduce global active-YT and PT liabilities consistently.
17. Redeem the matching strategy backing for 50 units of underlying.
18. Return the redeemed underlying to the market settlement flow.
19. Let the AMM retain the value required for the 50 PT it gave up.
20. Pay the remaining YT sale value to the user.
21. If `PayClaimableNow` was selected, pay the user's claimable yield from wrapper backing, not from AMM reserves.
22. Update reserves and verify all invariants.
23. Emit the checkpoint, burn, partial-close, and YT sale events.

## 9.3 Partial-sale example

At the halfway point:

```text
Active position before sale:       100 YT
Earned yield since last checkpoint:  3 USDC
User sells:                         50 YT
AMM values 50 PT at:              47.5 USDC
Gross underlying released:          50 USDC
YT sale value before fees:          2.5 USDC
```

The wrapper first credits or pays the 3 USDC earned while the user held all 100 YT. It then closes only 50 YT.

After the sale:

```text
Remaining active YT: 50
Remaining YT balance: 50
New settled index: current index at sale
Future yield accrues on: 50 YT only
```

The user's total immediate receipt, if `PayClaimableNow` is selected, is approximately:

```text
3.0 USDC accrued yield
+ 2.5 USDC YT sale value
- fees
```

## 9.4 Full YT sale

A full sale uses the same process with `yt_in == active_yt`.

Important rules:

- Accrued yield must be checkpointed before `active_yt` becomes zero.
- `claimable_yield` must not be deleted merely because the active amount becomes zero.
- If yield is not paid during the sale, the closed user must still be able to call `claim_yield()` later.
- Future yield accrual stops after the full close.
- The position record may be deleted only when both `active_yt == 0` and `claimable_yield == 0`, or it may be retained as historical state.

## 9.5 Failure behavior

The sale must revert if:

- The user lacks the required YT balance.
- `yt_in` exceeds the active position.
- The AMM lacks usable PT.
- The quoted output is below `min_usdc_out`.
- The matching PT/YT series differs.
- Blend withdrawal or wrapper redemption fails.
- A burn, transfer, or invariant check fails.

If `PayClaimableNow` fails only because yield liquidity is temporarily unavailable, the user should be able to retry with `CreditOnly` if the principal merge path itself remains possible.

## 10. Yield accrual and claiming

## 10.1 Source of truth

Earned yield must be calculated from the real strategy accounting value/index, such as Blend's on-chain rate, not from:

- The displayed APY.
- The AMM implied APY.
- An off-chain estimate.
- The YT market price.
- A manually configured yield number.

The exact formula depends on Blend's share/index representation and must use fixed-point arithmetic with documented rounding.

Conceptually:

```text
newly_accrued_yield
    = yield_between(settled_index, current_index, active_yt)
```

Then:

```text
claimable_yield += newly_accrued_yield
settled_index = current_index
```

## 10.2 When a YT holder may claim

In the recommended design, a valid YT position holder may claim:

- Any time after yield has accrued and before settlement.
- Immediately before a partial YT sale.
- Automatically during a partial YT sale.
- Immediately before a full YT sale.
- Automatically during a full YT sale.
- At or after series settlement for the final accrued amount.
- After fully selling YT if a previously checkpointed `claimable_yield` remains unpaid.

The user does not need to wait until maturity.

Claims should be rate-limited only for economic reasons such as minimum claim amount or transaction cost, not because the accounting cannot support multiple claims.

## 10.3 Claim function

Suggested interface:

```rust
claim_yield(
    user: Address,
    amount: Option<i128>,
) -> i128
```

Flow:

1. Require user authorization.
2. Load the user's series position or claim-credit record.
3. Confirm the user's YT balance backs all active YT liability, unless `active_yt == 0` and only historical claimable credit remains.
4. Read the current index, capped at the series settlement index when settled.
5. Checkpoint any newly accrued yield.
6. Determine the requested amount, defaulting to all claimable yield.
7. Verify the requested amount is positive and no greater than `claimable_yield`.
8. Verify backing and available payout liquidity.
9. Reduce `claimable_yield` before the external payout according to the contract's safe interaction pattern.
10. Transfer underlying yield to the user.
11. Increase `total_claimed`.
12. Update global unpaid-yield liabilities.
13. Emit a yield-claimed event.

If the payout transfer fails, Soroban transaction atomicity must leave the claimable balance unchanged.

## 10.4 Claim example with repeated claims

```text
Day 0:  User obtains 100 YT
Day 10: 2.0 USDC accrued; user claims 2.0
Day 15: 1.0 additional USDC accrued
Day 15: User sells 50 YT
        Wrapper checkpoints the 1.0 earned on the old 100-YT amount
Day 20: Remaining 50 YT earn 0.6 additional USDC
Day 20: User claims 0.6 plus any unpaid credited amount
```

The `settled_index` prevents the Day 0–10 and Day 10–15 yield from being claimed twice.

## 10.5 Claiming after a direct unsupported YT transfer

If Alice directly transfers her SAC YT to Bob outside Spield:

- Alice's wrapper position does not automatically move.
- Alice should fail the active-balance check.
- Bob has YT tokens but no corresponding wrapper position.
- Bob cannot claim Alice's historical or future yield through the position ledger.

The transfer can therefore strand the yield right. The UI, documentation, and token metadata should warn users clearly. This limitation remains until YT is redesigned as a programmable contract token with holder-based accounting.

## 10.6 Claim liquidity versus earned amount

The protocol must distinguish:

```text
Earned/accounting yield
Immediately withdrawable yield
Actually recovered underlying
```

A user may have valid claimable yield while Blend withdrawal liquidity is temporarily constrained. The claim should remain recorded and retryable rather than being erased.

Claims must never be paid by silently borrowing from PT principal backing or AMM trading reserves.

## 11. Maturity and settlement lifecycle

Recommended series states:

```rust
enum SeriesState {
    Open,
    CloseOnly,
    MaturedUnsettled,
    Settled,
    EmergencyPaused,
}
```

## 11.1 Open

- PT buys and sells are allowed within market limits.
- YT buys and sells are allowed within market limits.
- Yield claims are allowed.
- New splits are allowed.

## 11.2 CloseOnly

This optional state begins shortly before maturity or when risk limits are reached.

- New YT purchases are disabled.
- New splits intended only for leverage are disabled.
- YT sells may remain enabled if safe liquidity exists.
- PT trading may remain enabled within tighter bounds.
- Yield claims remain enabled.
- Direct merges remain enabled when strategy liquidity permits.

## 11.3 MaturedUnsettled

- New YT exposure is disabled.
- No new yield should be attributed beyond the defined settlement rule.
- A permissionless keeper or any caller should be able to trigger `settle_series()`.
- Trading should be disabled or restricted to a clearly audited settlement path.

## 11.4 Settled

- `settlement_index` is fixed.
- Future YT accrual is zero.
- YT holders can claim final earned yield.
- PT holders redeem principal directly from the wrapper.
- The AMM can enter LP settlement mode.

## 11.5 Exact maturity-index problem

If Blend exposes only the current index and no historical index, a transaction submitted after the maturity timestamp cannot reconstruct the exact rate at the earlier timestamp.

Spield must explicitly choose one of these rules:

### Rule A: First post-maturity settlement index

The first successful `settle_series()` call at or after maturity fixes the index. YT accrues until that call. This is simple and fully on-chain but may extend accrual slightly beyond the displayed timestamp.

### Rule B: Keeper-enforced near-exact settlement

A keeper calls settlement at the first eligible ledger. This reduces drift but still needs operational monitoring and incentives.

### Rule C: Verifiable historical index

Use a trustworthy historical-index mechanism if Blend and Stellar infrastructure can provide one safely. This adds complexity and an additional trust surface.

For the MVP, Rule A is the simplest defensible rule, but the UI and documentation must say that final accrual ends at on-chain series settlement, not an unattainable exact wall-clock instant.

## 11.6 PT redemption at settlement

PT redemption must depend only on:

- The holder's PT balance.
- The series settlement state.
- The wrapper's recoverable backing.

It must not depend on an original position or AMM reserve.

## 11.7 Final YT claim

Final YT claim uses:

```text
min(current_index, settlement_index)
```

After the settlement index is recorded, repeated calls cannot generate new yield.

## 12. AMM liquidity behavior and edge cases

## 12.1 Repeated YT buying

Each YT purchase adds PT and removes net USDC from the market:

```text
PT reserve rises
USDC reserve falls
PT price falls
YT price rises
```

Results:

- Later YT buyers pay more.
- Slippage increases.
- A reserve floor or price-range limit eventually rejects further YT buys.
- Existing YT is not liquidated.
- Claims remain independent.

## 12.2 Repeated YT selling

Each YT sale removes PT and returns net USDC to the market:

```text
PT reserve falls
USDC reserve rises
PT price rises
YT price falls
```

Results:

- Later YT sellers receive less.
- The market can run short of usable PT.
- YT exits may temporarily stop.
- Existing YT continues to earn and may still be held to settlement.

## 12.3 Equivalent directional trades

```text
YT buy  has a reserve effect similar to PT sell
YT sell has a reserve effect similar to PT buy
```

The market should use this relationship when designing dynamic fees and rebalancing incentives.

## 12.4 LP withdrawal during imbalance

LP withdrawal reduces available capacity. The AMM must:

- Allow only the LP's proportional entitlement.
- Recalculate usable reserves after withdrawal.
- Preserve minimum operational reserves if the pool design includes a lock.
- Never promise a user that early YT exit is guaranteed.
- Prevent an LP withdrawal from bypassing outstanding in-transaction locks.

## 12.5 Insufficient USDC reserve

If the pool cannot safely provide the USDC needed for a YT purchase:

- Return an explicit `InsufficientUsdcLiquidity` error.
- Do not use PT principal backing or claim reserves as a substitute.
- Do not partially fill an exact-output trade unless partial filling is explicitly designed and quoted.
- Suggest a smaller amount or a worse accepted maximum price in the UI.

### 12.5.1 USDC depletion is a liquidity condition, not liquidation

Repeated YT purchases and direct PT sales both move the pool in the same harmful direction:

```text
PT reserve increases
USDC reserve decreases
```

The pool has no collateralized debt, health factor, or liquidation price. A low USDC reserve must
therefore never trigger liquidation of a YT holder or an LP. Instead, the market must reduce and
eventually stop the USDC-consuming trade directions before the reserve becomes unsafe.

Receiving PT in exchange for USDC means the pool has exchanged one asset for another rather than
lost the USDC for no consideration. Nevertheless, the pool becomes less liquid and more exposed to
PT mark-to-market, wrapper, strategy, and maturity-redemption risk. A pool holding mostly PT may be
economically backed while being unable to service another early USDC-out trade.

### 12.5.2 Hard reserve and trade-cap checks

For each YT purchase, define:

```text
usdc_before       = authoritative stored USDC reserve
pool_usdc_needed  = curve-priced USDC contribution for the PT the pool receives
hard_usdc_floor   = configured minimum USDC that trading may not consume
post_usdc         = usdc_before - pool_usdc_needed
```

The trade may execute only if all of the following hold:

```text
pool_usdc_needed > 0
post_usdc >= hard_usdc_floor
pool_usdc_needed <= floor(usdc_before * max_trade_bps / 10_000)
net_usdc_out_in_window + pool_usdc_needed <= window_outflow_limit
```

The same capacity checks must include direct PT sales because they consume the same reserve. A user
must not bypass the YT-buy limit by performing the economically equivalent PT trade, or bypass a
per-transaction cap by splitting one large operation into many transactions in the same ledger or
configured rolling window.

The hard floor, per-trade cap, and rolling-window limit are governed risk parameters. They must be
bounded on chain, emitted in configuration-change events, and calibrated through liquidity and
economic simulations. An initially conservative policy may preserve approximately 20–30% of the
USDC reserve and limit a single transaction to approximately 2–5% of the current reserve, but these
figures are starting points for review, not universal constants to hardcode.

An exact-output YT purchase that exceeds the safe capacity must revert in full. Its quote should
return the maximum currently safe YT size so the UI can offer a smaller transaction without
pretending that the original amount can be filled.

### 12.5.3 Automatic liquidity modes

The market should derive or explicitly enter a liquidity mode from reserve utilization and risk
signals:

```rust
enum MarketLiquidityMode {
    Open,
    Guarded,
    CloseOnly,
    TradingHalt,
}
```

Recommended behavior:

| Mode | USDC-consuming directions | Rebalancing directions | Claims and maturity redemption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Open` | Allowed within normal limits | Allowed | Independent of AMM |
| `Guarded` | Smaller caps and higher imbalance fees | Allowed with normal or reduced fees | Independent of AMM |
| `CloseOnly` | New YT buys and direct PT sells disabled | PT buys and safe YT sells allowed | Independent of AMM |
| `TradingHalt` | Disabled | Disabled unless a separately audited recovery operation exists | Independent of AMM |

Entering `CloseOnly` because USDC is low must not stop PT buys or safe YT sells merely because they
use the same market contract; those directions add USDC and reduce PT inventory. Mode transitions
must be deterministic or governed under clearly bounded authority, observable in events, and
rechecked during execution rather than trusted from a frontend quote.

### 12.5.4 Rebalancing and recovery

The supported ways to restore USDC capacity are:

- Users buy PT with USDC.
- Users safely sell YT, causing the pool to exchange PT for USDC through the wrapper merge path.
- Existing or new LPs add PT and USDC at the required proportional ratio.
- Protocol-owned liquidity or an external market maker adds capital under the same disclosed LP
  accounting rules as other liquidity providers.
- At settlement, PT held by the pool or withdrawn by LPs is redeemed through the wrapper, assuming
  wrapper solvency and strategy withdrawal availability.

Dynamic fees may charge more for YT buys and PT sells when USDC is scarce and charge less for PT
buys and YT sells that restore balance. The direct PT and synthetic YT paths must share the same
reserve-transition and fee core so an actor cannot select a cheaper equivalent route.

The following are forbidden recovery mechanisms:

- Borrowing from wrapper principal backing.
- Using claimable-yield reserves.
- Taking assets from the fixed-rate vault.
- Minting unbacked PT or YT.
- Forcing an LP to absorb a non-proportional withdrawal or recapitalization.

### 12.5.5 LP withdrawals and reserve accounting

Trade reserve floors must not silently trap LP funds. An LP must retain the right to withdraw its
proportional PT and USDC entitlement under the documented LP rules, including during `CloseOnly` or
an emergency pause. After every LP withdrawal, the market must recalculate trade capacity and may
immediately move into a stricter liquidity mode.

Every YT purchase must verify both accounting and actual token movement:

```text
actual market USDC decrease == accounted USDC reserve decrease
actual market PT increase   == accounted PT reserve increase
actual post-trade reserves  satisfy the configured floors and limits
```

A direct token donation must not increase usable trading capacity until processed under the
market's audited donation/synchronization policy. Conversely, an unexplained actual-balance
shortfall must halt unsafe trading rather than being hidden by stored reserves.

### 12.5.6 Monitoring and user-facing behavior

Monitoring should alert on at least:

- USDC reserve and usable USDC above the hard floor.
- PT/USDC reserve skew.
- Per-ledger and rolling-window net USDC outflow.
- Current liquidity mode and recent transitions.
- Maximum safe YT purchase size.
- Difference between stored reserves and actual token balances.
- Failed YT purchases caused by the reserve floor or strategy availability.

The UI must describe this condition as **insufficient early-trading liquidity**, not liquidation or
loss of the user's existing YT. It should show the maximum safe size, suggest a smaller trade, and
explain that existing YT may still be held, claimed when strategy liquidity permits, or settled at
maturity.

## 12.6 Insufficient PT reserve

If the pool lacks matching PT for a YT sale:

- Return `InsufficientPtLiquidity`.
- Leave the YT position and claimable yield unchanged.
- Allow the holder to claim and continue holding until liquidity returns or settlement occurs.

## 12.7 Blend withdrawal constraints

An early YT exit requires the wrapper to recover underlying or otherwise settle equivalent value. If Blend withdrawal fails because available cash is low:

- The early exit may fail even when accounting backing exists.
- The position must remain unchanged.
- Claims already recorded must remain retryable.
- The UI should distinguish `StrategyLiquidityUnavailable` from AMM slippage.
- A protocol liquidity buffer may reduce, but not eliminate, this risk.

## 12.8 Blend loss or bad debt

If recoverable strategy assets fall below accounting assets:

- `PT + YT = 1 underlying` may no longer be fully realizable.
- The protocol must stop minting new liabilities.
- The protocol should pause unsafe early trading.
- A documented loss-allocation or haircut policy is required.
- The AMM price must not hide wrapper insolvency.

The contracts must not mint unbacked tokens to conceal a deficit.

## 12.9 USDC depeg, issuer freeze, or transfer failure

Spield is denominated in USDC units, not guaranteed external dollars. A USDC depeg affects the external value of PT, YT, reserves, and recovered yield. A freeze or authorization failure may prevent transfers.

Mitigations include:

- Explicit asset-risk disclosure.
- Emergency pause for new exposure.
- No reliance on a dollar oracle for ordinary accounting unless the protocol introduces cross-asset liabilities.
- Never silently replace USDC owed with another asset without an explicit settlement policy.

## 12.10 Price manipulation and sandwiching

A shallow AMM can be manipulated around a victim's transaction.

Required protections:

- `min_output` or `max_input` on every trade.
- Short deadlines or ledger-expiration limits.
- Maximum price impact per trade.
- Maximum reserve percentage per trade.
- No use of instantaneous AMM price for solvency, mint caps, or claim calculation.
- Careful review of multi-operation transactions that move price before mint/merge.

## 12.11 Donation and reserve desynchronization

Anyone may transfer tokens directly to a contract address. If quotes sometimes use stored reserves and sometimes use raw balances, donations can create inconsistencies.

The AMM must define one audited policy:

- Stored reserves are authoritative and donations are separately recoverable, or
- Reserves are synchronized in a controlled function with explicit rules.

Never allow a caller to create a favorable quote by donating a token and exploiting inconsistent pre/post-balance calculations.

## 12.12 Rounding and dust

Required rules:

- Use a single fixed-point scale across wrapper and market calculations.
- Specify whether each operation rounds in favor of solvency.
- Reject outputs below a minimum economically meaningful amount.
- Prevent repeated dust trades from extracting rounding value.
- Ensure partial sells cannot leave an unclaimable one-unit position unless explicitly allowed.
- Sum-of-parts tests must not create more redeemable value than a single equivalent transaction.

## 12.13 Near-maturity trading

Near settlement, YT value approaches zero and rounding becomes proportionally important.

Recommended controls:

- Disable new YT buying at a configured cutoff before maturity.
- Allow safe close-only exits while liquidity exists.
- Reject trades whose fair YT value is below the dust threshold.
- Tighten price bounds as maturity approaches.
- Disable all ordinary YT swaps once the series is matured.

## 12.14 Direct unsupported YT transfer

The AMM must never treat possession of SAC YT alone as sufficient proof of a sellable yield position.

For a YT sale, verify both:

```text
user YT balance >= amount being burned
user active wrapper position >= amount being closed
```

This prevents a recipient of stranded, directly transferred YT from extracting a claim that the wrapper has not assigned to them.

## 12.15 Multiple positions or vault custody

A vault may hold aggregate YT for many beneficiaries. A simple balance check does not identify which beneficiary owns which yield.

Before supporting vault-routed YT trading, define:

- Whether the vault is the wrapper position owner.
- Whether users hold internal vault shares rather than YT.
- How the vault checkpoints before deposits, withdrawals, or reallocations.
- How aggregate YT balance backs total beneficiary liabilities.
- Who receives yield during partial vault exits.

Do not reuse the direct-user balance check without verifying vault accounting.

## 12.16 Authorization failure

Every user-debit operation must require the user's authorization. Every market-funded split must verify the registered market. Administrative functions must be separated from normal trading permissions.

Failure must revert without modifying:

- Position amount.
- Claimable yield.
- AMM reserves.
- Global liabilities.

## 12.17 Re-entry and call-graph safety

Do not implement:

```text
Wrapper → YT transfer hook → Wrapper checkpoint
```

Do not implement a flash callback that re-enters an already executing market or router.

Prefer a one-direction call graph:

```text
User → Market → Wrapper → Strategy/token contracts
```

Receiving PT, YT, or USDC must not invoke the receiving Spield contract again.

## 12.18 Pauses and upgrades

Pause permissions should be granular:

- Pause new splits.
- Pause YT buys.
- Pause early YT sells.
- Pause PT trading.
- Preserve claims when safe.
- Preserve maturity redemption when safe.

An emergency pause must not automatically confiscate or erase claims. Upgrade authority and token administration must be disclosed, secured by multisig/timelock where practical, and tested independently from classic issuer lockdown.

## 12.19 State archival and TTL

Series, position, claim-credit, and configuration entries must have an explicit Soroban TTL-extension policy. A matured but unclaimed position may remain dormant for a long time and must not become practically inaccessible because its storage was not extended or restored.

## 12.20 Transaction simulation versus execution

Reserves and Blend state may change after frontend simulation but before execution. All slippage, deadline, state, maturity, and reserve checks must therefore run inside the contract during execution.

## 13. Rebalancing and market availability

The pool should encourage trades that restore balance.

| Pool state | Harmful direction | Rebalancing direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Too much PT, too little USDC | YT buy or PT sell | PT buy or YT sell |
| Too little PT, too much USDC | PT buy or YT sell | PT sell or YT buy |

Possible tools:

- Dynamic fees based on imbalance.
- Better execution prices for rebalancing flow.
- Protocol-owned seed liquidity.
- External market-maker incentives.
- Limit orders in a later release.
- Per-series reserve targets.
- Trade-size throttles.
- Temporary close-only mode.

These mechanisms improve availability but do not create a guarantee of early exit.

## 14. LP economic exposure

LPs hold a changing combination of PT and USDC.

When users buy YT, LP inventory moves toward PT. When users sell YT, LP inventory moves toward USDC. LPs therefore bear:

- Interest-rate and implied-yield price risk before maturity.
- Inventory imbalance risk.
- Early-withdrawal mark-to-market risk.
- Wrapper and Blend risk through PT.
- USDC risk.
- Smart-contract and administrative risk.
- Opportunity cost if the USDC reserve is idle rather than yield-bearing.

LPs are not normally liquidated because the AMM does not leave a collateralized debt position. They may nevertheless realize a loss when withdrawing early or during an underlying-loss event.

If technically feasible in a later version, Spield may evaluate a PT/standardized-yielding-base pool rather than PT/raw-USDC, similar in purpose to a PT/SY market. This can reduce idle-capital cost but adds wrapper and redemption complexity and requires a separate design review.

## 18. Required tests

## 18.1 PT trading tests

- Buy PT with exact input.
- Sell PT with exact input.
- Slippage failure.
- Expired deadline.
- Zero, negative, dust, and maximum amounts.
- Repeated PT buys until PT reserve limit.
- Repeated PT sells until USDC reserve limit.
- PT transfer between unrelated holders followed by maturity redemption.

## 18.2 YT buy tests

- User pays only the quoted YT value.
- AMM provides the remaining underlying.
- PT is minted directly to the correct AMM.
- YT is minted directly to the correct user.
- User position begins at the current index and earns no historical yield.
- Existing user position is checkpointed before adding new YT.
- Insufficient pool USDC reverts everything.
- Blend supply failure reverts everything.
- PT mint failure reverts everything.
- YT mint failure reverts everything.
- Position-write failure reverts everything.
- Post-trade invariant failure reverts everything.
- Maximum trade size and reserve floor work.

## 18.3 Full and partial YT sale tests

- Sell all YT.
- Sell exactly half of YT.
- Sell one unit below full amount.
- Reject more than active amount.
- Checkpoint uses the old full amount before reduction.
- Accrued yield remains claimable after a full close.
- Remaining YT accrues only from the new settled index.
- AMM supplies exactly matching PT.
- Mismatched series reverts.
- Insufficient PT liquidity reverts without changing the position.
- Blend withdrawal failure reverts without changing the position.
- `CreditOnly` and `PayClaimableNow` behave consistently.

## 18.4 Yield claim tests

- Claim before maturity.
- Claim repeatedly with no double payment.
- Claim immediately before partial sale.
- Automatic checkpoint during partial sale.
- Claim after partial sale.
- Claim during full sale.
- Claim after full sale when credited yield remains.
- Final claim after settlement.
- No accrual after settlement index.
- Zero-yield interval.
- Very small index change and rounding.
- Strategy rate regression/loss path.
- Insufficient withdrawal liquidity preserves claimable amount.
- Direct unsupported YT transfer blocks the original owner's active claim.
- Recipient of directly transferred YT cannot claim without a position.

## 18.5 Pool stress tests

- One hundred consecutive YT buys.
- One hundred consecutive YT sells.
- Alternating PT and YT trades.
- YT buys combined with PT sells to stress USDC reserve.
- YT sells combined with PT buys to stress PT reserve.
- A YT buy that would cross the hard USDC floor reverts without changing any balance or position.
- A direct PT sale cannot bypass the hard USDC floor used by YT purchases.
- A large trade split across one ledger or rolling window cannot bypass the cumulative USDC-outflow limit.
- A trade exactly at the hard USDC floor succeeds and the next USDC-consuming unit is refused.
- Per-trade maximum reserve percentage is enforced at its exact boundary.
- The quote returns a maximum safe YT size that executes without crossing the floor.
- `Guarded` and `CloseOnly` modes activate at their configured thresholds.
- `CloseOnly` blocks YT buys and PT sells while allowing PT buys and safe YT sells.
- Rebalancing trades restore USDC capacity and return the market to a less restrictive mode.
- Dynamic fees penalize the depleting direction and favor the rebalancing direction consistently.
- LP withdrawal at balanced reserves.
- LP withdrawal at extreme skew.
- LP withdrawal remains proportional and available while the trade reserve floor is active.
- LP withdrawal recalculates capacity and can move the market immediately into `CloseOnly`.
- Dynamic fee and reserve-floor behavior.
- Trade at the maximum allowed price impact.
- Token donation followed by quote and trade.
- A token donation does not increase usable capacity before an authorized reserve synchronization.
- An actual-balance shortfall relative to stored reserves halts unsafe trading.
- Stored-reserve versus actual-balance consistency.

## 18.6 Maturity tests

- Trade one ledger before cutoff.
- Reject new YT buy at cutoff.
- Close-only YT sale at cutoff.
- First valid settlement call.
- Repeated settlement call is idempotent.
- Final yield index cannot change.
- PT bearer redemption without original position.
- LP settlement.
- Dust YT near maturity.
- Delayed settlement under the chosen maturity-index rule.

## 18.7 Security and authorization tests

- Unregistered market attempts pool-funded split.
- Fake PT, YT, wrapper, and underlying addresses.
- Cross-series merge.
- Replayed or expired authorization.
- Malicious caller tries to burn another user's YT.
- Nested-call/re-entry attempts.
- Paused-market bypass attempts.
- Admin changes and upgrade authorization.
- Issuer-lockdown rehearsal.
- Storage archival and restoration for dormant positions.

## 18.8 Property and invariant tests

Use randomized/property tests to verify:

- Successful splits always increase PT and YT liabilities equally.
- Successful merges always decrease matching liabilities equally.
- No trade decreases the AMM invariant beyond explicitly permitted fee/rounding rules.
- No sequence of partial claims and sells pays more yield than the strategy generated for that position.
- Splitting a trade into many small transactions cannot extract more value through rounding than the permitted tolerance.
- Global active YT equals the sum of active user/vault YT liabilities.
- Global unpaid yield equals the sum of claimable credits.
- Maturity redemption cannot exceed recoverable principal backing.

## 20. User-facing behavior summary

The interface should communicate the following clearly.

### Buying PT

> Pay USDC and receive transferable PT. Hold until maturity for principal redemption or sell earlier at the market price.

### Selling PT

> Sell transferable PT into the market. Early-sale value depends on liquidity, time, and implied yield.

### Buying YT

> Pay only the current market value of future yield exposure. The market supplies the remaining split capital and receives PT. YT is minted directly into your Spield yield position.

### Claiming yield

> Claim yield already generated by your active YT before maturity. Claims use actual strategy yield and do not reduce your remaining YT amount.

### Partially selling YT

> Spield first records all yield earned by your old full YT amount, then closes only the amount you sell. The remaining YT continues earning from the new checkpoint.

### Fully selling YT

> Spield records all yield earned up to the sale, burns the full YT position, and pays the market value of the remaining future-yield exposure. Previously earned yield remains payable even after the active position reaches zero.

### Liquidity warning

> Early PT and YT trades depend on market liquidity and are not guaranteed. Earned-yield claims and maturity settlement do not depend on AMM reserves, although they remain subject to underlying strategy and asset availability.

### Transfer warning

> Direct YT transfers outside Spield are unsupported in this version and may strand the associated yield rights.

## 21. Final architectural recommendation

Spield can launch with a single PT/USDC market and capital-efficient YT routing without forcing a user to provide the full principal, provided the market itself participates atomically in the split/merge flow.

The core design is:

```text
PT buy:
User USDC → AMM → User PT

PT sell:
User PT → AMM → User USDC

YT buy:
User pays YT value
+ AMM supplies remaining underlying
→ Wrapper deposits full underlying
→ PT minted to AMM
→ YT minted to user

YT sell:
Wrapper checkpoints seller's accrued yield
+ AMM supplies matching PT
+ User burns chosen YT amount
→ Wrapper releases underlying
→ AMM retains PT value
→ User receives YT sale value

Yield claim:
Wrapper checkpoints real Blend yield
→ pays or credits user from strategy backing
→ never uses AMM reserves

Maturity:
Series fixes settlement index
→ YT receives final earned yield
→ PT bearer redeems principal
→ AMM is not required
```

This design removes temporary-principal capital requirements for users and avoids ordinary liquidation, but it cannot guarantee unlimited early liquidity. Pool imbalance, Blend withdrawal availability, underlying loss, and USDC risk must be treated as explicit availability or solvency risks rather than hidden behind the AMM.
